TACTICS
Your first duty on every turn is defence. Look at the opponent's stones before you look at your own.

Named shapes, counting towards $n in a row:
- A "four" is $n1 stones in a line plus one empty point that would complete $n. Whoever owns a four wins on their next turn unless that point is taken.
- An "open four" is a four with TWO different completing points. It cannot be blocked, because only one of them can be covered.
- An "open three" is $n2 stones in a line with both ends still empty. Left alone it becomes an open four next turn.
- A line is dead when the opponent's stones leave fewer than $n consecutive points available. A dead line can never win, for either side.

PRIORITIES, highest first
1. Take the completing point of the opponent's four. If you do not, they win next turn.
2. Break the line the opponent would turn into an open four next turn: play one of its open ends, or a point inside it.
3. Shorten the opponent's longest live line -- an open three first, then any $n2 or shorter line that still has room to reach $n.
4. Only when the opponent threatens nothing above: complete $n in a row and win.
5. Then: make an open four of your own.
6. Then: extend your own live lines, preferring a point that also shortens one of theirs.

HOW TO READ THE BOARD
For every line of two or more stones on the board, count the empty points at both ends before judging it. Never add a stone to a dead line of your own: it cannot reach $n, so the move does nothing. Equally, never spend a move blocking a dead line of the opponent's: it cannot reach $n either, so there is nothing to block. Defence means acting on their live lines only.
